Basement Waterproofing Solutions for Lenexa, KS Homes

By the Lenexa Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026.

The right basement waterproofing solution depends on where the water comes from. Grading and gutters fix surface water, interior drain tile and a sump pump handle groundwater at the floor, and crack repair stops wall leaks.

This guide compares the main options and shows how to find the source before you spend money, for homes in Lenexa, KS.

Find the source before you pick a fix

Check the outside first

Look at gutters, downspouts and whether the ground slopes toward the house.

Note where it shows inside

Floor-wall joint, mid-wall crack or one corner each point to a different cause.

Note the timing

Water only after heavy rain points to groundwater or surface water. Water in dry weather suggests a plumbing leak.

Test for condensation

Tape foil to the wall for a day. Moisture on the outside of the foil is condensation.

Each fix solves a different problem.

Signs that point to each source

Use these clues alongside the table.

  • Damp along the floor edge after rainGroundwater pressure is pushing up at the joint.
  • A streak under one crackA wall crack is letting water through.
  • Water near a window wellA clogged well drain or poor grade is the likely cause.
  • White chalky depositsWater has moved through the masonry and left salts behind.
  • Moisture that appears regardless of weatherA plumbing leak or condensation is more likely.

Interior drainage and a sump pump: how they work together

An interior drain collects water at the footing and sends it to a sump pit. A pump then moves the water outside. The system does not keep water out of the wall. It gives the water a controlled path before it reaches the floor.

Battery backup matters. A system that stops when the power goes out will fail in the storm that tests it. We cover installation on our sump pump page.

The discharge must go outside and away from the foundation, which the next section explains.

Drainage you can fix cheaply before spending big

Many wet basements start outside. Gutters packed with leaves overflow beside the foundation, and downspouts that end at the wall pour water right where it does the most harm. Extend downspouts several feet from the house and clean gutters in fall and spring.

Check the grade as well. Soil should slope away from the foundation, and settled backfill near the wall often leaves a low spot that holds water. Adding soil and compacting it is inexpensive compared with an interior system.

Window wells need working drains and covers. A well that fills with rain can leak through the glass or the frame.

Lenexa notes: do not send water into the sewer

Johnson County Wastewater says stormwater from sump pumps, downspouts and area drains that reaches the sanitary sewer can overload it and cause sewage backups. The county's voluntary JoCo Inflow program has inspected more than 55,000 properties since 1983 and removed about 17,000 stormwater sources.

A waterproofing system that discharges into the sewer solves one basement and can contribute to another. Call JoCo Inflow at 913-715-8550 for a free evaluation if you are unsure.

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can tell you whether interior drainage or pump work needs a permit.

Older homes and newer homes

Lenexa was incorporated in 1907, and housing ranges from older neighborhoods to new construction. Listing records place areas such as Whispering Hills in the 1970s and 1980s and Bristol Ridge in the 2010s. Foundation age and drainage design vary, so a fix that works on one street may not suit another.

Older foundations often show cracks and settled grading. Newer ones may have drain tile that has clogged or a pump that is undersized. We look at what your foundation actually has.

Similar variety exists in Overland Park, which we also serve.

A note on mold and health

Persistent moisture can lead to mold growth. We do not make health claims about mold exposure, and a physician is the right person to talk to about symptoms.

What we can say is that drying a wet area quickly, within a day or two, limits growth, and fixing the source is the only lasting solution. A dehumidifier helps with humidity but does not stop a leak.

If you suspect a hidden plumbing leak, call. We find them with specialized equipment and without opening walls where possible.
