Installing a water heater the right way in a Lenexa home

A good installation starts with the space. A heater needs clearance to breathe, a floor that can handle a flood, and a path for gas, vent or wire. In many Lenexa houses with unfinished basements, the heater sits near a floor drain, which makes placing a drain pan and discharge line straightforward.

Lenexa requires basements and bedrooms to have egress, so many homes have finished lower levels. A heater in a finished basement closet needs more care, because an unnoticed leak can damage drywall and carpet before anyone sees it. We add a pan and discharge route in those spots.

If you already know the tank has failed and need it replaced fast, the water heater replacement page covers that path.

Planning checklist before the installer arrives

A few minutes of preparation make the visit smoother.

  • Clear the pathMove boxes and shelving so the old tank can come out and the new one can go in without hauling stored items upstairs.
  • Locate your shutoffsFind the main water valve and the gas shutoff, and tell us if either is stuck or missing.
  • Note the old tank detailsTake a photo of the rating label. It shows capacity, fuel and manufacture date.
  • Think about hot water useCount people at home and list any soaker tub or large shower. That sets the right capacity.
  • Ask about optionsHeat pump, tankless and high-efficiency tank units each have different venting and electrical needs.

Gas, venting and the codes Lenexa has adopted

Lenexa adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), the 2018 International Mechanical Code and the 2018 International Fuel Gas Code in Ordinance 5890. Those set the rules for relief valves, vent connectors, combustion air and gas sizing, and they are the standard our installs follow.

Before work begins, confirm whether your project needs a permit with the Lenexa Community Development office at 913-477-7725 or permits@lenexa.com. A permit holder must be licensed, and the City asks for a Lenexa business license and a Johnson County Contractor's License. You can look up a contractor at cls.jocogov.org.

Expansion tanks, relief valves and the parts people skip

A closed plumbing system cannot expand back into the street main, so heating water raises pressure. An expansion tank absorbs that swing. Many older installs have none, and a failed relief valve is the usual reminder.

We include a new relief valve with a full-size discharge line, new flexible connectors, and a pan in locations where leaks would reach finished floors. These parts cost little compared with the water damage they prevent.
