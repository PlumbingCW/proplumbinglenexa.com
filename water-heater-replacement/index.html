Water heater replacement is the right call when the tank itself fails

A water heater that leaks from its body, not from a fitting, has lost its glass lining. The steel under it corrodes, the seam opens, and the tank fills the floor again no matter what you patch. At that point you are choosing a replacement, not a repair.

The other trigger is age. Most tank heaters in Johnson County homes are replaced around the 10 to 12 year mark, sooner on a poorly maintained unit. We read the serial number on arrival so the age is a fact, not a guess.

If the problem is only an element or a thermostat, our water heater repair page covers when a repair makes sense.

Failed tank: safety steps while you wait

Do these in order while we dispatch.

  1. Close the cold water shutoff on the heater inlet. If it will not turn, close the main water valve for the house.
  2. Turn off the electric breaker or set the gas control to off. If gas is smelled, leave the building and call 911 first.
  3. Do not walk through a flooded basement near electrical panels or outlets.
  4. Pull stored items out of the water path and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  5. Open a hot faucet upstairs to relieve pressure only if the shutoff is closed.

Choosing the replacement: size, fuel and type

One visit is usually enough to choose well. We help with these decisions.

  • Tank sizeHouseholds of two to three people often use a 40 to 50 gallon tank. Larger families or soaking tubs push toward 60 or 75 gallons.
  • Fuel typeReplacing gas with gas or electric with electric is the simplest. Changing fuel means new gas piping, venting or a new circuit.
  • EfficiencyA higher-efficiency tank lowers operating cost, and Lenexa has adopted the 2012 International Energy Conservation Code, which our installs follow.
  • Heat pump or tankless optionsBoth cost more up front. We explain the trade-off and the venting or electrical work each needs.
  • Where it sitsBasements make drain and pan placement easier. Garage and closet units need clearance checks.

Code, permits and who may do the work in Lenexa

Lenexa adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), the 2018 International Fuel Gas Code and the 2017 National Electrical Code (NEC) through Ordinance 5890. Those documents set the rules for relief valves, gas piping, venting and the electrical connection on a new heater.

For a city permit, the City requires contractors to hold a Lenexa business license and a Johnson County Contractor's License. You can check any contractor at cls.jocogov.org or call Johnson County Contractor Licensing at 913-715-2233. Ask the Community Development office at 913-477-7725 which permit applies to your job.
