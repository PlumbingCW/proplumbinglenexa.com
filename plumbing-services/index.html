Choosing the right plumbing service

Plumbing problems fall into a few groups. Active water, sewage and gas issues are emergencies and go to the first group. Drains and sewer lines are the second group, and heaters and installations are planned work that benefits from a clear quote.

A few quick pairings help. A slow main line usually starts with drain cleaning, and a camera check through sewer inspection follows when clogs return. Hot water that fails after age 10 is more likely a water heater replacement than a repair. A rising water bill with no visible leak calls for leak detection.

What to have ready when you call

A few details save time. Know where the main water valve is, what the problem looks like and whether anything is still running. Take a photo of any leak or damage, and write down the age and fuel type of a water heater if that is the issue.

For a drain or sewer problem, note which fixtures are affected and whether it happens in rain. That tells dispatch whether to send cable machines, a jetter or a camera. If you have already tried a plunger or an auger, say so.

If the problem is in a rental, call the landlord or property manager first, since they usually decide who repairs it. If you own the home, you are in charge of the decision, and we can talk through the options.

Local rules that touch most jobs

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), the 2018 International Fuel Gas Code and the 2017 National Electrical Code (NEC), which cover plumbing, gas piping and related wiring. Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can tell you which jobs need a permit.

Johnson County licenses contractors, and you can search any contractor at cls.jocogov.org or call Johnson County Contractor Licensing at 913-715-2233. Johnson County Wastewater runs the public sewer, and JoCo Inflow at 913-715-8550 helps with rain-driven backups.

Seasons that bring the most calls

Hard freezes bring frozen and split pipes, and our burst pipe repair page covers what to do the moment one lets go. Spring storms bring failed sump pumps and wet basements, covered on the sump pump and basement waterproofing pages.

Hot water trouble tends to show up on the coldest mornings, when a tired heater finally quits. If yours is more than about ten years old, ask about replacement before it fails at night.
