Who Installs a Sump Pump? A Lenexa, KS Homeowner Guide

By the Lenexa Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026.

A licensed plumber is the usual choice to install a sump pump, because the job involves a pit in the slab, a discharge line and a check valve. A basement waterproofing contractor or an electrician may take part when the project includes drains or new wiring.

This guide explains who does what, what to ask before you hire and where the pumped water has to go in Lenexa, KS.

Which contractor does which part

Several trades can be involved.

TradeWhat they doWhen you need them
PlumberSets the pit, pump, check valve and discharge line.Most installs and replacements.
Basement waterproofing contractorInstalls interior drain tile and larger drainage systems.When water enters at the wall-floor joint or walls.
ElectricianAdds a dedicated circuit or GFCI outlet.When no suitable outlet is nearby.
Concrete or restoration crewPatches the floor after cutting.After a new pit or drain tile.
Landscaper or excavatorFixes grade and extends discharge outdoors.When surface water is the source.

What a full installation includes

A new installation starts with the lowest point of the slab. The crew cuts the concrete, digs a pit, sets a liner and places the pump on a level base. A discharge line runs to the outside with a check valve so water does not drain back into the pit.

The float is adjusted so the pump starts and stops at the right level. The crew then fills the pit to test the pump and checks the discharge point outdoors.

A battery backup or water-powered backup can be added. We also recommend a sealed lid to control moisture and radon entry paths.

Questions to ask before you hire

A good answer to each is a sign of a careful contractor.

  • Are you licensed in Johnson County?Check at cls.jocogov.org or 913-715-2233.
  • Where will the water discharge?It should go outside and away from the foundation, never into the sewer.
  • What backup do you recommend?A pump without backup fails in a power outage.
  • Is a permit needed?The contractor should know and handle it where required.
  • What does the warranty cover?Ask about the pump and the labor.

What a sump pump install costs you in time and disruption

A pump replacement into an existing pit is usually a short visit with little mess. A new pit is different. The crew cuts a square of concrete, removes the soil and sets the liner, so expect noise, dust and a patched section of floor afterward.

Clear the area around the install spot before the crew arrives. Move stored items away from the work zone and make sure there is a path to the outdoor discharge point. If the basement is finished, ask the crew how they will protect carpet and walls.

Ask for a written description of the work, the pump model and the backup option so you can compare bids. A good quote names the parts and the discharge route, not just a total.

Where the water has to go in Lenexa

Johnson County Wastewater says stormwater entering the sanitary sewer from sump pumps, downspouts and area drains can overload it. The county's voluntary JoCo Inflow program has inspected more than 55,000 properties since 1983 and removed about 17,000 stormwater sources.

Call JoCo Inflow at 913-715-8550 for a free evaluation. If a connection is found, repairs carry a one-year warranty from the plumbing contractor, according to the program.

A properly installed pump discharges outdoors. Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can tell you whether a permit applies to your project.

Finished basements and new construction

A finished basement raises the stakes, since carpet, drywall and furniture are at risk. If you are finishing a basement, install the pump and drain system first and the finishes second.

Newer neighborhoods such as Bristol Ridge from the 2010s often have drain tile and a pit already installed during construction, while older areas such as Whispering Hills from the 1970s and 1980s may not. Check what you have before assuming.

Homes in Overland Park vary in the same way, and we serve both cities.

When the pump is not the real fix

A sump pump handles water that reaches the pit. It does not stop water that enters through wall cracks or a leaking pipe. If water appears at the middle of a wall or comes in during dry weather, look elsewhere.
