Most Efficient Tankless Water Heater for Lenexa, KS Homes

By the Lenexa Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026.

Among tankless water heaters, condensing gas models and electric models post the highest efficiency ratings, but the best choice for a Lenexa home depends on the fuel you have, the size of the household and the cost of gas and electricity.

This guide explains how to read the efficiency label, compares the main types and shows what else decides whether a unit saves money.

How to read the efficiency rating

The rating to compare is the Uniform Energy Factor, or UEF. It is a number on the unit's label and the yellow EnergyGuide sticker that reflects how much of the fuel becomes hot water in typical use. Higher is better.

Do not compare a UEF number against an older Energy Factor number from a different test. They are measured differently, and mixing them leads to wrong conclusions.

A high rating does not guarantee a low bill. Fuel prices, how much hot water you use and the unit's sizing all change the result.

Condensing gas, standard gas and electric compared

Each type has a different efficiency profile and a different set of requirements.

TypeEfficiency profileWhat it needs
Condensing gas tanklessHighest among gas units because it recovers heat from exhaust.Plastic venting and a condensate drain, plus a properly sized gas line.
Non-condensing gas tanklessLower than condensing, still better than many tanks.Metal venting and a gas line sized for a high burner rating.
Electric tanklessVery high at the unit because almost all electricity becomes heat.A large dedicated circuit and panel capacity.
Heat pump tank heaterHighest overall efficiency of any type, but it is a tank, not tankless.Floor space, airflow and a condensate drain.

Heat pump tank models are not tankless, but they are worth a look if efficiency is your main goal.

What else decides whether a unit saves money

The label is only the start.

  • Fuel cost where you liveNatural gas and electricity price per unit of heat differ. Check your latest bills.
  • Household hot water useHeavy use makes an efficient unit pay back faster.
  • Correct sizingA unit that is too small or too large wastes energy or underperforms.
  • Installation qualityPoor venting or a short gas line cuts real-world performance.
  • MaintenanceA scaled-up heat exchanger loses efficiency, so flushing matters.

Sizing: the step most buyers skip

Efficiency does not help if the unit cannot keep up. Tankless units are rated in gallons per minute at a given temperature rise, so list the fixtures you might run at once and the coldest incoming water you expect in winter. Choose a unit that meets that flow with margin.

A rise that is too high for a small electric unit means lukewarm showers in January. A larger gas unit is often the answer for a home with several baths.

A professional load calculation also checks whether your gas line or electrical panel can supply the unit, which is often where the real cost sits.

Scale and maintenance protect the rating

The efficiency printed on the label assumes a clean heat exchanger. Mineral scale insulates the metal and forces the unit to work harder, so a unit with hard water and no maintenance slowly loses the efficiency you paid for.

WaterOne, which supplies Lenexa, publishes an annual water quality report with hardness data, and you can test your own tap. Use the result to set a flushing schedule and decide whether a softener belongs in the plan. Our water softener and filtration page explains the next steps.

Annual flushing is a routine job described on our tankless water heater page.

Lenexa notes: code, permits and who installs it

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the 2017 National Electrical Code (NEC). A tankless installation usually involves gas piping, venting or a new circuit, so ask Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 which permits apply before you buy.

Use a contractor licensed through Johnson County and confirm it at cls.jocogov.org or 913-715-2233. Gas venting and electrical work are the two areas where mistakes are most costly.

Homes in newer areas such as Bristol Ridge may already have a gas line sized for more demand, while older neighborhoods such as Whispering Hills often need an upgrade. We also serve Overland Park and see the same range of homes.

Our practical take on picking one

If you have gas service and room to vent, a condensing gas unit is usually the efficient choice with the lowest running cost. If your panel is strong and you have no gas, a properly sized electric unit is clean and simple. If you want maximum efficiency and have the space, ask about a heat pump tank heater too.

Do not chase the top number alone. A correctly sized, properly vented and regularly flushed mid-range unit often beats an oversized premium unit that is installed poorly.
