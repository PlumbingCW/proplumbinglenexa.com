Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Lenexa, KS

A sewer line that has cracked, shifted or filled with roots will back up again no matter how many times it is cleared. We find the damage with a camera and repair it in Lenexa, KS, and our emergency line answers all night.

Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage in a tub, shower or basement floor drain is a health issue as well as a repair. Stop running water, avoid contact with the overflow and keep children and pets away. A dishwasher or washing machine cycle only pushes more water into the line.

Call us. A crew can clear the line to restore service and then camera it to see why it failed. Clearing is a stopgap, not a cure, if the pipe is damaged.

If the backup happened during heavy rain, stormwater may have entered the sewer through a private connection. That is a different problem, and Johnson County has a program for it, described below.

Signs the line needs repair, not another cleaning

A cleaning fixes a clog. A repair fixes the pipe. These signs point to a repair.

  • Backups that return within monthsA clean pipe that clogs repeatedly usually has a crack, belly or root entry catching debris.
  • Roots shown on a camera passRoots enter at joints and cracks. Cutting them only buys time.
  • A belly in the lineA low spot holds water and solids, which a cable cannot fix.
  • Soil or sinkholes above the lineSoil entering a broken pipe leaves voids that show up in the yard.
  • Sewer smell in the yard or basementGas escaping through a crack can reach the house or lawn.

Cast iron, clay and plastic: the pipe under your lawn

Older sewer laterals were often cast iron or clay, and both age poorly. Cast iron scales inside and can crack outright. Clay joints open and let roots in. Newer homes use PVC, which resists roots but can sag if bedding is poor.

Lenexa has grown quickly since 1970 and the subdivisions range from 1970s ranch and split-level homes to new construction. Listing records place areas such as Whispering Hills in the 1970s and 1980s and Bristol Ridge in the 2010s, so the pipe material under your lot depends on when your house was built.

We identify the pipe on camera and match the repair to the material, because a clay repair and a PVC repair use different parts.

When rain, not roots, causes the backup

Johnson County Wastewater runs JoCo Inflow, a voluntary program that has inspected more than 55,000 properties since 1983 and removed about 17,000 stormwater sources connected to the sewer. Sump pumps, downspouts, area drains and uncapped cleanouts sending rain into the sewer can overload it and back sewage into basements.

If backups occur only during heavy rain, call JoCo Inflow at 913-715-8550. The evaluation is free, and repairs carry a one-year warranty from the plumbing contractor.

Septic systems work differently from a public sewer, and this page covers homes connected to Johnson County Wastewater. If your property uses septic, county environmental staff are the right contact.
