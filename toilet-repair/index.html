Emergency Toilet Repair in Lenexa, KS

Toilet overflowing: do this now

This sequence keeps water off the floor.

  1. Reach behind the bowl and turn the shutoff valve clockwise. If the valve is stuck, lift the tank lid and raise the float by hand.
  2. Do not flush again. A second flush adds more water to a bowl that is already full.
  3. Put towels around the base and open a window if the odor is strong.
  4. If water keeps rising and reaches other fixtures, the main line is blocked. Call us for drain cleaning.
  5. Call dispatch. Tell us whether the water is clear tank water or a sewage backup.

Reading the symptoms: what is wrong with the toilet

Most toilet trouble falls into a handful of causes.

  • Water runs after the flushA worn flapper or a misadjusted fill valve is the usual cause. Left alone, it wastes water and raises your bill.
  • Water around the baseA failed wax ring, a cracked flange or a loose bolt lets water escape at the floor.
  • A bowl that rocksA loose toilet strains the seal. Tighten it carefully or reset it with a new wax ring.
  • Weak flushClogged rim jets, a partly blocked trap or a low tank fill can reduce flush power.
  • A hairline crack in the tankA cracked tank leaks slowly. Epoxy rarely holds, and replacement is the sound fix.

Wax rings and flanges: the repair hidden under the toilet

Every toilet sits on a wax ring that seals the bowl to the drain flange. When the ring fails, water seeps onto the floor, and the subfloor can rot unseen. A sour smell near the base is often the first clue.

We pull the toilet, inspect the flange, replace the ring and set the bowl on new bolts. A cracked or corroded flange needs a repair ring or replacement so the toilet sits level and secure.

If the subfloor feels soft, we tell you before we reset the toilet, because a rotted floor needs repair first.

Leak at the base of the toilet?

Close the shutoff and call. A leaking seal rots the subfloor, so quick action matters.

Installing a new toilet

A new toilet is a small remodel with a few decisions. Rough-in distance, which is the measure from the wall to the flange center, decides which models fit. Height, bowl shape and flush type then follow.

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which governs how fixtures are connected and vented. A straight swap of a toilet usually does not need a permit, but moving a toilet or adding a bathroom does. Ask Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725.

If you are remodeling a bathroom and need faucets and valves changed as well, see our faucet repair page or plumbing installation for larger projects.
