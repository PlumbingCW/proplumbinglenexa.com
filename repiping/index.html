When repair stops making sense and repiping starts

One leak in a pipe is a repair. A third leak in a year is a pattern. When pipes corrode from the inside, the weak spots keep appearing and every patch makes the next failure more likely.

Repiping replaces the supply lines that carry water to fixtures. It does not cover drain lines, which are a separate system, and we say so up front so the scope is clear.

We look at the pipe material, the number of leaks, water pressure and your plans for the house, and then tell you whether a partial repipe or a whole-house repipe makes more sense.

Signs your supply pipes need replacing

Several of these together make a strong case.

  • Repeated pinhole leaksEach patch holds until the next weak spot opens up.
  • Rust-colored or metallic waterGalvanized steel corrodes from inside and sheds rust.
  • Low pressure at every fixtureScale and corrosion narrow the pipe over the years.
  • Green or blue stains on copperSurface corrosion often hints at thinning walls.
  • Polybutylene pipeThis gray plastic used in some homes from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s is prone to failure, and homeowners often choose to replace it.

PEX, copper or CPVC: how the options compare

The right material depends on your house and budget.

MaterialStrengthsTrade-offs
PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, fast to install, resists scale.Must be protected from sunlight and some chemicals.
CopperLong track record, strong, handles heat well.Costs more and can corrode in aggressive water.
CPVCLower material cost, resists corrosion.Can become brittle with age and is less forgiving to install.

We follow the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Lenexa has adopted when choosing and joining pipe.

Before a repipe starts: what to do to get ready

A little preparation keeps the job smooth and the house safe.

  1. Clear the areas where walls will be opened, including closets, under sinks and the water heater room, so the crew can reach the pipes.
  2. Find the main water shutoff and show it to everyone in the house. If a pipe fails before the crew arrives, close it and call.
  3. Plan for water to be off for part of each day. Fill containers for drinking and cooking, and arrange showers elsewhere if needed.
  4. Move valuables, art and fragile items out of rooms with planned wall openings, and keep pets away from work areas.
  5. Do not use chemical drain cleaners or open a wall yourself. Hidden wiring and old pipe make guessing dangerous.

Lenexa homes and the pipe inside them

Lenexa was incorporated in 1907 and grew quickly from the 1970s on. Homes of that period are the ones most likely to contain older copper, galvanized steel or polybutylene, while newer houses typically have PEX.

WaterOne supplies drinking water to Lenexa and publishes an annual water quality report. It also offers a Service Line Lookup Tool and states that it has no record of lead service lines in its distribution system. The lookup tool shows the material of the line from the meter to your house.

We identify what is inside your walls before quoting, because the plan for a 1980s house is different from the plan for a house built in the 2010s.

Permits and what to expect during the work

Repiping a house normally needs a plumbing permit. Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can confirm what applies to your address, and Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Expect the water to be off for part of the day, access holes in walls and closets and some dust. We protect floors, cover furniture and tell you which fixtures will be out of service at any time.

If an access hole falls in a finished room, we patch it so a painter can blend it, but we do not repaint. Plan for that if you want the wall to look untouched.
