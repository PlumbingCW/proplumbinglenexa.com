Emergency Leak Detection in Lenexa, KS

Most people do not call about a leak. They call about a water bill, a meter that will not stop, or a warm spot on the floor. We find hidden leaks in Lenexa, KS using acoustic, thermal and pressure methods so repairs target the right spot.

If water is actively flowing, close the main shutoff and call. Otherwise, the steps below help you narrow it down while you wait.

Start with the meter: the free test you can do now

Turn off every faucet, the ice maker and the washing machine. Look at the meter. If the low-flow dial is still turning, water is moving somewhere in the pipe between the meter and your fixtures.

Now shut the main valve inside the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside your house. If it keeps turning, the leak is on the service line between the meter and the foundation.

WaterOne, the water utility for the Johnson County area, notes that it has no record of lead service lines in its distribution system and offers a Service Line Lookup Tool that shows your service line material. That tool helps us understand the pipe we are testing.

Symptoms that point to a hidden leak

Leaks announce themselves in odd ways. These are the signs that justify a detection visit.

  • A water bill that jumped without explanationCompare three months. A steady climb with no change in habits suggests a slow leak.
  • A meter that runs with fixtures offThis is the clearest sign of a leak somewhere downstream.
  • Warm spots on a floorA hot water line leaking under slab or in a wall can warm the surface above it.
  • Wall or ceiling stainsBrown rings on drywall, bubbling paint or a musty smell suggest moisture inside the cavity.
  • Sound of running water at nightA hiss or trickle when everything is off often means a pressurized pipe is leaking.

Ceiling, wall, crawlspace and under-house leaks

A ceiling stain is rarely right below the leak. Water travels along joists and drops where the surface first gives way. We trace it back with a moisture meter and thermal camera, check supply lines, drain lines and the roof above, and only then decide where to open.

Many Lenexa homes have basements, and the City of Lenexa requires egress in finished and unfinished basements and bedrooms. A leak in a finished basement ceiling can be a supply line, a drain, or the shower above it, and we test each separately.

Underground and buried-line leaks need a different approach, and our locate work for those is described in the water line paragraph below.

Yard, irrigation and water service line leaks

A leak between the meter and the house shows up as a soft spot in a lawn, a puddle in a dry stretch of yard, or a meter that runs when the house is off. Irrigation lines are a second source, especially after a hard freeze.

We shut the main inside the house to confirm the leak is on the buried line, then pinpoint it acoustically before digging. If the service line needs replacement, we explain the options and any permit steps under the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Lenexa has adopted. Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can confirm what applies.

Pool and spa leaks are a different system, and we focus on household plumbing here.
