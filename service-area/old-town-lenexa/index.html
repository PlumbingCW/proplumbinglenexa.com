Where Old Town Lenexa sits and what it is

Civil engineer Octave Chanute platted the town in 1869 after buying 41.5 acres near the railroad right of way from a local farmer, Charles A. Bradshaw. The City says the original plat was one block along Santa Fe Trail Drive, and the Lenexa Historical Society placed marble markers at the plat corners in 1996.

Today the name covers a wider area of surrounding neighborhoods. The City does not publish exact boundaries, so we do not draw one here. If you are not sure whether your address counts, the street name and your cross street are what matter on a service call.

The Old Town Lenexa Activity Center campus, formerly the Lenexa Community Center and Senior Center, is a well-known landmark. A BNSF rail line also runs through the area.

What building age does and does not tell us

Many Old Town buildings are decades old, and some are over a century old. Age alone does not tell us what pipe is inside a wall. Over the years owners add bathrooms, replace sections and patch lines, so one building can hold several materials.

We identify what is actually there before recommending anything. That might mean a quick look at the supply lines, a camera pass on the drain, or a pressure check. If a repair turns into a bigger job, we explain why before work starts.

When pinhole leaks or repeated repairs point to worn supply piping, the repiping page explains your options. For a hidden wet spot, start with leak detection.

Water and sewer: who handles what in Old Town

WaterOne supplies drinking water in Lenexa and publishes an annual water quality report. Its Service Line Lookup Tool shows the service line material at an address, and WaterOne states that it has no record of lead service lines in its system.

Johnson County Wastewater runs the public sewer. The line from your building to the public main is generally the owner's. A camera pass shows which side a blockage is on, and if it is on the public main you can reach Johnson County Wastewater at 913-715-8600.

Permits and older-building work

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can tell you whether a water heater swap, a repipe or a sewer repair needs a permit. The City page on Old Town does not describe a separate plumbing rule, so confirm what applies to your address before work starts.

Ask any contractor to show the license they hold. Johnson County Contractor Licensing can be reached at 913-715-2233, and licensed contractors can be searched at cls.jocogov.org.
