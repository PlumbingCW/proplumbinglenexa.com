Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in Overland Park

Do these in order. They take a few minutes and limit the damage.

  1. Close the main water valve, usually where the line enters the basement or utility room near the meter, by turning it clockwise.
  2. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines and relieve pressure.
  3. Switch off the breaker to wet areas only if you can reach the panel from a dry spot, and keep clear of standing water near outlets.
  4. Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  5. Call our 24/7 line and give the street, the nearest cross street and what failed. Dispatch gives you an arrival window and a clear price before billable work begins.

What Overland Park homeowners call us about most

Overland Park is Johnson County's largest city and was incorporated in 1960, so homes here run from mid-century houses to recent construction. That spread explains the call mix. Older lots tend to bring clogged drains, aging supply lines and tired water heaters. Newer lots bring finished basements and the sump systems that protect them.

Drain stoppages come first. A kitchen line that backs up on a holiday, a tub that rises when the washer drains or a basement floor drain that bubbles all point to a blocked line, and our drain cleaning crews clear them with cable machines and jetters.

Water heater failures come next. A tank that leaks from the base needs replacing, and a heater with no hot water may only need a part. We tell you which before we quote, and the water heater repair page explains how we decide.

Sewer backups, rain and the Johnson County system

Johnson County Wastewater runs the public sewer that serves Overland Park. If a floor drain backs up during heavy rain, stormwater may be entering the sewer through private connections. Johnson County's voluntary JoCo Inflow program has inspected more than 55,000 properties since 1983 and removed about 17,000 stormwater sources, and you can reach it at 913-715-8550.

The lateral from your house to the public main is generally your responsibility, and a camera pass shows whether the blockage sits on your side or the county side. We document the footage so you can call Johnson County Wastewater at 913-715-8600 if the problem is on the public main.

A line that backs up more than once a year is a candidate for a camera inspection.

Overland Park water supply and what it means for your pipes

WaterOne, the water district that serves most of Johnson County, supplies Overland Park homes and publishes an annual water quality report. Its Service Line Lookup Tool shows the material of the service line at an address, and WaterOne states that it has no record of lead service lines in its distribution system.

Your house plumbing is a separate matter. Older copper, galvanized steel and polybutylene all show up in homes of different ages, so we identify the material before quoting a repair. If you keep finding pinhole leaks, ask about repiping.

Mineral buildup is a common complaint. We do not guess your hardness. Check the WaterOne report, or test the tap yourself, and see our water softener page for the next step.

Permits and licensed work in Overland Park

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), but permit rules in Overland Park are set by that City, not by Lenexa. Before a water heater replacement, a repipe or a sewer repair, call City Hall at 913-895-6000 and ask which permit and inspection apply to your address.

Hire a contractor who holds the proper license and can show it on request. In Johnson County you can search licensed contractors at cls.jocogov.org or call Johnson County Contractor Licensing at 913-715-2233.

We explain what permits apply before we start, so you are not surprised at the end of the job.
