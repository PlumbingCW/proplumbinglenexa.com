Plumbing failure at your business: first steps

A calm first ten minutes protects staff, customers and the building.

  1. Shut off the fixture or the zone valve that feeds the problem. If you do not know which one, close the building main.
  2. Keep people out of wet or sewage-contaminated areas and put up a sign if customers may enter.
  3. Photograph the damage for your insurance claim before cleanup starts.
  4. If a restaurant or food area is affected, stop food prep in the zone and ask your local health authority about reopening rules.
  5. Call us and tell dispatch the building type, the failure and the nearest access point.

Commercial plumbing is a different job from residential

Commercial buildings have larger pipes, higher flow, multiple restrooms and equipment such as grease interceptors, backflow preventers and commercial water heaters. A mistake takes a bigger share of the business with it.

Lenexa has grown into a business center, with large employers and industrial and office space throughout the city. Pipe layouts in a strip center, a warehouse and a restaurant look nothing alike, so we match the crew to the building.

We handle repair, maintenance and installation. Our scope is plumbing, and we coordinate with other trades when a job crosses over.

Commercial plumbing problems we see most

Some show up in every kind of building.

  • Floor drains backing upGrease, wipes and debris in a main line block it, and a floor drain is the first place it shows.
  • Restroom fixtures that run or leakFlush valves and faucets get heavy use and wear faster than residential ones.
  • Water heater failureA commercial water heater serves many fixtures, so a failure is felt everywhere.
  • Low pressure on upper floorsCorroded risers or failing valves can starve upper levels.
  • Slow drains in food areasKitchen lines clog with grease, and a hydro jetter often clears them fully.

Permits, codes and who is responsible

Commercial plumbing work generally needs permits, and the City of Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can explain which permits and inspections apply to your project.

For a lease, check who is responsible for what. In many leases the tenant handles fixtures inside the unit and the landlord handles the building systems, but terms vary. We document findings so you can show a landlord or insurer exactly what failed.

Johnson County Wastewater serves the public sewer, and a camera pass shows whether a blockage sits on your lateral or on the public main.

Maintenance that prevents the next emergency

Scheduled maintenance costs less than an emergency. A grease line cleaned on a schedule does not back up in the middle of dinner service, and a water heater that is flushed and inspected lasts longer.

We can set up recurring visits for cleaning and inspection, and we leave a written note on what we found so the next crew has a history to work from.

For long, repeat problems, a camera inspection gives a clear picture of the main line before you commit to a repair.
