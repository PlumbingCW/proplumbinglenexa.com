A leaking or dead disposal: the first five minutes

Switch the disposal off at the wall. Never put your hand into a disposal, even when it is off, because it can start when jammed parts shift.

If water is on the floor, look for the source. A leak from the top flange is different from a leak from the body, a drain connection or the dishwasher hose. A leak from the body means the unit has failed and replacement is the practical fix.

If the kitchen sink is also backed up, the problem is beyond the disposal, and a kitchen line clog needs a cable. Our drain cleaning page explains what we do.

Disposal symptoms and what they usually mean

Different symptoms lead to different repairs.

  • Humming but not turningThe motor has power but a jam holds the flywheel. Many jams clear with the hex key under the unit.
  • Silence at the switchCheck the reset button on the bottom. If it will not reset, the motor or wiring has failed.
  • Leak at the bottom or sidesInternal seals have failed, and the unit needs replacing.
  • Leak at the sink flangeThe mounting gasket or plumber putty has failed and can often be resealed.
  • Smell or slow drainingFood buildup in the line or a partly blocked trap may be the cause.

Installing a garbage disposal

A disposal needs a power source, usually a switched outlet under the sink, a discharge connection to the trap and a dishwasher connection if you have a dishwasher. If there is no outlet or switch, an electrician may need to add one. Lenexa has adopted the 2017 National Electrical Code (NEC), which sets the rules for that wiring.

Installation follows a clear order. We remove the old unit, set the mounting assembly, connect the discharge and dishwasher hose, and test for leaks and noise. The dishwasher knockout plug inside the unit must be removed, or the dishwasher will not drain. We always confirm this step.

If your sink needs a new faucet at the same time, see our faucet repair page.

What not to put in a disposal

Grease, coffee grounds, pasta, rice, potato peels, bones and fibrous vegetables such as celery cause most jams and clogs. Grease coats the drain and hardens, and starchy foods swell into paste.

Run cold water before, during and for a few seconds after the disposal. Citrus peels and ice may freshen the unit but do not clean the drain line.

If your kitchen line repeats clogs despite careful use, ask about a camera and hydro jetting to see what is in the pipe.
