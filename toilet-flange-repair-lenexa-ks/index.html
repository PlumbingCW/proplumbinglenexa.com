Toilet Flange Repair in Lenexa, KS: When a Repair Ring Works

By the Lenexa Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026.

The toilet flange is the ring on the floor that the toilet bolts to and that connects to the drain. When it cracks, rusts or sits too low, the toilet rocks and the wax ring cannot seal. A repair ring or kit fixes many flanges, but a rotted floor or a broken pipe needs more.

This guide explains how to spot a bad flange, what the repair kits do and when to call a plumber in Lenexa, KS.

Signs a toilet flange has failed

The symptoms show up at the base of the toilet.

  • The toilet rocksA loose toilet strains the seal and can move on a cracked flange.
  • Water around the baseA failed wax ring leaks, and a damaged flange will not hold a new one.
  • A sewer smell near the toiletGas escapes when the seal is broken.
  • A soft or stained floorSlow leaks rot the subfloor around the flange.
  • Bolts that will not holdA cracked or rusted flange cannot anchor the closet bolts.

What the flange does and how it fails

The flange anchors the toilet to the floor and aligns the bowl with the drain. A wax ring or a rubber gasket sits between the bowl and the flange to seal the connection.

Flanges are made of plastic, cast iron, brass or galvanized steel. Cast iron rusts, plastic cracks, and a flange that sits below the finished floor after a remodel will not compress the wax ring correctly.

The flange can also pull loose from the pipe or the subfloor, which is a structural problem and not a ring problem.

Leak at the base of the toilet?

Close the shutoff and call. A leaking base rots the subfloor, so quick action matters.

Repair ring, extender or full replacement

The right fix depends on what is damaged.

ConditionFixNotes
Cracked or broken top of the flangeRepair ring or flange repair kit.The ring screws to the floor over the damaged flange.
Flange below the finished floorFlange extender or a thicker ring.Brings the seal height up to the toilet.
Corroded cast iron flangeReplace or sleeve the flange.May involve the drain pipe.
Rotted subfloorRepair the floor first.Otherwise nothing will hold.
Flange loose from the pipeReplace or reconnect.A job for a plumber, see toilet repair.

How a flange repair is done

Shut off and remove the toilet

Close the supply, drain the tank, disconnect and lift the toilet.

Inspect the flange and floor

Look for cracks, rust, a loose flange and soft wood.

Install the repair

Fit the ring or replace the flange and anchor it securely to a sound floor.

Set with a new wax ring

Set the toilet on a fresh ring and bolts and tighten evenly.

Test

Flush several times and check for leaks and rocking.

Why repair kits sometimes fail

A repair ring is only as strong as the floor under it. If the subfloor has rotted, the screws will not bite, and the toilet will rock again. A ring also cannot fix a drain pipe that is broken below the flange.

Wax rings are single use. If you lift the toilet for any reason, replace the ring. A reused ring often leaks.

If you are not sure what is under the toilet, a plumber can inspect before the toilet is reset. That avoids a second failure.

Checking the flange height yourself

Lift the toilet and look at where the top of the flange sits relative to the finished floor. Ideally it rests on top of the floor surface or slightly above it. If it sits below the floor, the wax ring cannot compress and the seal fails.

Measure from the floor to the top of the flange with a ruler. A flange that sits lower than the surface, even a quarter of an inch, usually needs an extender or a thicker ring.

While the toilet is off, stuff a rag into the drain opening to block sewer gas and keep tools from falling in. Remove the rag before you reset the toilet.

Lenexa notes: older bathrooms and remodels

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC). A like-for-like toilet reset usually does not need a permit, but moving a toilet or changing the drain does. Ask Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725.

Remodels in older Lenexa homes often add a new floor over the old one, which can leave the flange below the finished floor. Areas such as Whispering Hills from the 1970s and 1980s see this often, while newer homes such as Bristol Ridge from the 2010s are less likely to.

When to call a plumber

Call if the toilet leaks, if the floor feels soft or if the flange looks broken, rusted or loose. These point to damage a kit cannot fix.

A plumber can also check the drain below the flange with a camera if backups are happening. See our sewer inspection page.

If you just need a quick reset and the floor is solid, a kit may be all you need.
