What plumbing installation covers

Installation work means adding or moving something that is not there now. A new sink, a washer hookup, an outdoor spigot, a bathroom in the basement and a gas line for a range all count.

The plan starts with the drain. Water supply can be routed almost anywhere, but a drain needs slope, venting and a place to go. That is why we look at the drain path before we talk about fixtures.

We install the work, test it under pressure and leave you with a system that passes inspection.

Permits, inspections and Lenexa code

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), the 2017 National Electrical Code (NEC) for related wiring and a local set of amendments. Plumbing work that adds or moves fixtures generally needs a permit, and a like-for-like swap often does not.

Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can tell you what your project needs. We tell you in advance which permits apply and what inspections to expect, so you are not surprised later.

Using a licensed contractor matters. Johnson County licenses contractors, and unlicensed work can cause problems when you sell.

Gas lines and safety

Gas piping is a different category of risk. A leak can cause fire or explosion, and an unsafe connection can create carbon monoxide. We pressure test every new gas connection and check for leaks before leaving.

If you smell gas, do not turn on lights or use a phone near the smell. Leave the house, then call the gas utility and 911 from outside. Our emergency crew can follow once the utility has cleared the area.

For appliance hookups like a range, dryer or water heater, we confirm the shutoff, the connector and the venting before we call the job done.

Basement bathrooms and below-grade drains

Basement bathrooms add real value, but they depend on the drain depth. If the main drain is deeper than the fixture, gravity does the work. If not, a sewage ejector pit pumps waste up to the main line.

We check the depth, the slab and the vent path before quoting. A pump system needs power, a sealed lid and a vent, and the pit must be accessible for service.

If the basement also has moisture problems, deal with them first. Our basement waterproofing page explains how.
