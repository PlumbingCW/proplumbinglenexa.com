When a faucet or valve leak is an emergency

A dripping faucet is annoying. A faucet that will not shut off, a burst supply line or a shower valve that sprays inside the wall is an emergency. The stop valve is the first defense, and if it fails, the house main is next.

Water that runs inside a wall can saturate drywall within hours. If you hear running water with every faucet closed, check the shutoffs and the meter, and if the leak continues, call for leak detection too.

We answer faucet and valve emergencies 24/7 and carry common cartridges, stops and supply lines on the truck.

Faucet and valve problems we see most

Each symptom points to a different part.

  • Drip from the spoutA worn cartridge, seat or O-ring lets water pass when the faucet is closed.
  • Leak around the handleA worn packing or cartridge seal leaks at the stem.
  • Water under the sinkThe supply line, compression fitting or the faucet base may be leaking.
  • Low flow at one faucetA clogged aerator or a partly closed stop valve restricts flow.
  • Tub faucet that will not stop drippingA tub spout or valve cartridge has worn out, and the repair is inside the wall.

Shutoff valves and supply lines: small parts, big floods

Under every sink and behind every toilet sits a shutoff valve. Older multi-turn valves seize with mineral buildup or leak at the stem, and old braided supply lines can burst without warning. These are inexpensive parts that prevent expensive floods.

When we replace a faucet, we usually replace the shutoff and the supply lines at the same time, because you do not want to be back under the sink in a year.

A burst supply valve is covered in our burst pipe repair page for lines larger than a fixture connection.

Shower and tub valves: the repair behind the wall

Showers and tubs have a mixing valve inside the wall. When the cartridge fails, the handle may drip, the temperature may swing or the tub may leak from the spout. Some valves can be repaired from the front with a new cartridge. Others need access through the wall.

We try the front-access repair first. If the valve body is corroded or an old model, we open the wall from the least visible side, replace the valve and patch the opening. We also look for signs of earlier leaks.

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets rules for water temperature control valves in showers, and we follow it on valve replacements.
