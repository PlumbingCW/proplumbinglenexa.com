About Lenexa Pro Plumbing

Lenexa Pro Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing line for Lenexa, KS and nearby Johnson County towns. It exists to connect a homeowner with a licensed plumber quickly and honestly.

This page explains how that works and how to check any plumber you hire.

What we do

We answer the phone around the clock and route plumbing emergencies and planned jobs to licensed plumbers working in Lenexa and nearby towns. Dispatch asks what is happening, tells you what to shut off, and gives you a window and a clear price before billable work begins.

Our service list covers emergency repairs, drains, sewers, water heaters, leak detection and installs. See the full list on our plumbing services page.

How to verify any plumber in Lenexa

Do not rely on a logo or a website. The City of Lenexa requires contractors who pull permits to hold a Lenexa business license and a Johnson County Contractor's License. You can search licensed contractors at cls.jocogov.org or call Johnson County Contractor Licensing at 913-715-2233.

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and for permit questions you can contact the Lenexa Community Development office at 913-477-7725 or permits@lenexa.com. They can tell you which projects need a permit and which inspections follow.

What we promise and what we do not

We promise a clear price before work starts and same-day appointments when our schedule allows. We do not promise a fixed arrival time, because traffic, weather and other emergencies change a day. If the problem is in the public sewer main, we say so and point you to Johnson County Wastewater at 913-715-8600.

We also do not give legal or insurance advice. If water damage reaches floors or walls, photograph it and call your insurer early.

How a call works from start to finish

You call the 24/7 line and a dispatcher answers. The dispatcher asks what is happening, where in the house it is and whether water or sewage is on the floor. If there is something you can shut off, such as the main water valve or the supply to a fixture, the dispatcher walks you through it while the crew is arranged.

Next you get an arrival window and a clear price before billable work begins. The crew arrives, finds the cause and explains the repair in plain terms. You approve the work, the crew finishes and tests it, and you get a walkthrough of the shutoffs so you know what to do next time.

Emergency work and planned work

Emergency work means water you cannot stop, sewage coming up, no heat or hot water in the cold or a gas smell. Those calls go to the front of the line, and our pages for burst pipe repair, sewer line cleaning and water heater repair explain what to do before we arrive.

Planned work covers things like a new fixture, a repipe or a tankless water heater conversion. Those jobs are easier to price and schedule, and they benefit from a walkthrough of the house first.

How pricing works

You get a price before billable work starts. The price depends on what we find, so we do not quote a flat number over the phone for a problem we have not seen. Each service page lists the factors that move the price, such as access, parts and the time of the call.

After-hours visits carry a rate that dispatch states first. If the repair turns out to be larger than expected, the crew explains the change and waits for your approval before going ahead.

Questions people ask before they call

Can you tell me the price on the phone? For a simple, well-described job dispatch can give a range of what the work involves, but the firm price comes after the crew sees the problem. You approve it before billable work begins.

What if I am not sure it is an emergency? Call and describe it. If water is moving, sewage is rising or there is a gas smell, treat it as an emergency. If it can wait until morning, dispatch will say so and book a time that suits you.

Will I get the same crew twice? Not always. We keep notes on what was found so a second visit does not start from zero.

Why our pages name local offices and utilities

Plumbing advice is only useful if it fits where you live. Our pages name the offices that really decide things in Lenexa, such as Lenexa Community Development for permits, WaterOne for drinking water and Johnson County Wastewater for the public sewer.

If a fact on one of our pages ever looks out of date, call the office named on the page. They are the source, and we would rather you check than guess.
