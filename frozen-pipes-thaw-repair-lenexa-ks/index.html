Frozen Pipes in Lenexa, KS: How to Thaw and Repair Them

By the Lenexa Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026.

If no water comes out of a faucet in freezing weather, a pipe has probably frozen. Open the faucet, heat the cold section gently with a hair dryer and watch for leaks as the ice melts. Never use an open flame.

If the pipe has already split, close the main water valve and call. The guide below covers both cases and how to keep it from happening again.

No water at the tap in freezing weather: what to do first

Act in this order.

  1. Find which faucets are affected. If only one is dry, the freeze is likely local. If all are dry, check the main line near the meter.
  2. Leave the faucet open. As the ice melts, water needs a place to go, and an open tap relieves pressure.
  3. Locate the main water shutoff before you start, so you can close it fast if a pipe leaks as it thaws.
  4. Apply gentle heat to the frozen section with a hair dryer, warm towels or a heating pad, starting near the faucet and working back toward the frozen spot.
  5. Never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame. Heat that is too strong can split pipe or start a fire.

Why pipes freeze and where they split

Water expands as it turns to ice. The ice itself does not always split the pipe. The pressure trapped between the ice plug and a closed faucet often does, and the split can sit well away from the freeze.

That is why a pipe can look fine while frozen and start spraying when it thaws. Be ready at the main valve as the ice melts.

Pipes most at risk are in exterior walls, garages, crawl spaces, unheated basement corners and under sinks on outside walls. Outdoor spigots and sprinkler lines are also common victims.

Signs a pipe has frozen or cracked

Check for these when the weather turns hard.

  • A trickle or no flow at one tapIce is blocking the line, often in an exterior wall.
  • Frost on an exposed pipeThe pipe temperature has reached freezing.
  • A bulge or a visible crackThe pipe has been stressed or has split.
  • A wet spot after the thawWater is escaping from a split you could not see before.
  • Odd smells from a drainA frozen drain line can hold waste and block flow.

Repairing a split pipe

A split pipe needs the damaged section cut out and replaced. Copper is repaired with new copper and solder or push-fit couplings. PEX is repaired with fittings and crimp rings. We check the whole line, since a freeze often damages more than one spot.

Water damage is a separate matter. After the leak is stopped, wet drywall and insulation should be dried quickly, since mold can begin to grow within a day or two. Photograph everything before it is moved, and talk to your insurer.

If the same pipe has frozen before, consider rerouting it or insulating the wall cavity. Chronic freeze-ups may justify a repipe of the vulnerable section.

Preventing a repeat freeze

Insulate pipes in exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces with foam sleeves. Seal air leaks that let cold air reach pipes. Keep the heat on and set the thermostat no lower than a safe minimum when you travel.

On a hard freeze, open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls and let a faucet drip slightly. Moving water is harder to freeze.

Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze.
