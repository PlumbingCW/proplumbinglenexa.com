Whole House Water Filtration Systems: A Lenexa, KS Homeowner Guide

By the Lenexa Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026.

A whole house water filtration system treats the water where it enters the house, so every tap and appliance gets filtered water. Which filter you need depends on what is in your water, and the only reliable way to know is to test.

This guide covers the main filter types, what the certification labels mean and what to check before you buy for a home in Lenexa, KS.

Call (855) 575-2890Read the FAQ

Start with a problem, not a product

People buy whole house filters for different reasons. Some want better taste and odor. Some want to protect plumbing and appliances from sediment. Some are worried about specific contaminants. Each goal points to a different type of filter.

A filter is not a cure-all. A sediment filter will not remove dissolved minerals, a carbon filter will not soften water and a softener does not filter drinking water. Naming the problem first keeps you from buying the wrong thing.

Write down what you notice and where, then get a test.

Filter types and what each one does

Here is a plain comparison.

Filter typeHelps withDoes not do
Sediment filterSand, silt and rust particles.Remove dissolved chemicals or minerals.
Carbon filterChlorine taste and odor and some organic compounds.Soften hard water.

Reading the certification label

Labels from independent testers matter more than marketing claims.

  • NSF/ANSI 42Covers aesthetic effects such as chlorine taste and odor.
  • NSF/ANSI 53Covers health-related contaminants the product lists.
  • NSF/ANSI 58Applies to reverse osmosis systems.
  • Specific claims onlyA certification covers only what the data sheet lists, so check the list.
  • Replacement filter availabilityA system is only as good as the filters you can keep buying.

Test before you buy

WaterOne, which supplies Lenexa, publishes an annual water quality report listing what it tests for and the results. Read the latest report first, then test the tap in your house for the specific issue you care about. A lab test shows what the utility report cannot, such as what your own plumbing adds.

If you have a private well or another supply, test that water directly, since the utility report does not cover it.

Bring the result to us and we will match the equipment to the numbers, not to a sales brochure.

Where the system goes and what the install involves

A whole house system goes on the main line after the meter and shutoff, and ahead of the water heater. It needs room for the tanks or housings, a bypass so you can isolate it and, for some types, a drain and an outlet.

Pressure matters. A filter that restricts flow can starve showers on upper floors, so we check flow ratings against what the house needs.

Filters need service. Cartridges and media must be replaced on a schedule, and skipping it can make water worse than before. We explain the schedule before the install.

Maintenance and what happens if you skip it

Every filter needs care. Sediment cartridges clog, carbon media loses capacity and softener resin wears out. A clogged filter can cut flow to showers and appliances, and an exhausted carbon filter can stop removing taste and odor without any warning.

Set a reminder for each change on the day you install the system. Keep the manufacturer instructions and note the date of each service on the housing with a marker.

If water changes in taste or smell after months of good performance, check the filter age first. It is the most common reason for a sudden change.

Lenexa notes: utility service line and permits

WaterOne offers a Service Line Lookup Tool that shows the material of the service line at your address, and it states that it has no record of lead service lines in its distribution system. Check the tool for your address.

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and connecting treatment equipment to the main line may need a permit. Ask Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725, and use a contractor licensed through Johnson County, listed at cls.jocogov.org.

Our practical advice

Most homes do not need an elaborate system. Many do well with a sediment filter and a carbon filter, plus a softener if the water is hard and a reverse osmosis unit at the kitchen sink for drinking water.

Be wary of any seller who recommends a system without testing your water. If the pitch comes with pressure or a deadline, walk away and call us.
