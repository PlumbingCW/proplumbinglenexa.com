Tankless Water Heater Installation, Repair and Flush in Lenexa, KS

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so it needs correct gas supply, venting and regular descaling to keep working. We install, repair and flush tankless units in Lenexa, KS for houses, townhomes and condos.

This page covers all three jobs. It also answers whether tankless is the right choice for your home.

How a tankless water heater actually works in your house

A tankless heater fires a burner or electric element the moment water flows, then shuts off when the flow stops. There is no stored hot water, so output is limited by flow rate and temperature rise, not tank volume.

That is why a unit that works well for two showers can struggle when a third fixture opens in winter, when incoming water is colder. Sizing matters more than with a tank.

Gas models need correct line size, a proper vent and a condensate drain on high-efficiency units. Electric models need a lot of amperage, often more than an older panel offers.

Flush and descale: the maintenance most owners miss

Heat drives minerals out of water, and they coat the heat exchanger as scale. Scale reduces flow, raises error codes and shortens the life of the unit. A flush runs a descaling solution through the exchanger and rinses it clean.

WaterOne, which supplies treated water in the Johnson County area, publishes an annual water quality report with hardness values and explains that you divide total hardness in ppm by 17.1 to get grains per gallon. Pull the latest report, or test your tap, to see how aggressive scaling is likely to be at your address.

Flush frequency depends on water hardness and use. Many manufacturers call for an annual flush, and we follow the manual for your model.

Signs a tankless unit needs service

These symptoms usually show up before the unit stops.

  • Error codes that returnMany codes relate to flow, ignition, venting or scale. Writing down the code speeds diagnosis.
  • Cold water sandwichHot, then cold, then hot again can mean a flow sensor, a scaled exchanger or a mismatched recirculation setup.
  • Lower hot water flowScale or a clogged inlet filter reduces the flow the unit sees.
  • Noise during firingRumbling or popping can come from scale in the exchanger.
  • Exhaust or condensate problemsA plugged vent or a clogged condensate line can shut the unit down.

Gas line, venting and code for tankless installs

Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Fuel Gas Code and the 2018 International Mechanical Code, which cover gas piping and venting for appliances like these. Tankless units draw a high burst of gas, so a line sized for a tank heater is often too small.

We calculate the total connected load, check the vent run and clearances, and confirm the condensate route before quoting. For permit questions, call Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725.

If a tankless unit is fighting you and you would rather go back to a tank, our water heater installation service covers that conversion.
