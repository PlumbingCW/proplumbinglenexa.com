How to Install a Garbage Disposal: A Lenexa, KS Homeowner Guide

By the Lenexa Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026.

Installing a garbage disposal means mounting the unit under the sink flange, wiring or plugging it in, connecting the discharge to the trap and linking the dishwasher hose. An experienced DIYer can do it in a few hours if the sink already has power nearby.

This guide walks through the steps, the parts you need and the points where a Lenexa homeowner should stop and call a licensed pro.

What you need before you start

Check four things first. Is there a switch for the disposal, and is there an outlet or a junction box under the sink? Does the sink have a standard 3.5 inch drain opening? Is the sink cabinet deep enough for the unit? And do you know where the main water shutoff is, in case something goes wrong?

Buy the right unit for your use. Horsepower, noise insulation and feed type vary, and a higher horsepower unit handles tougher loads. A continuous feed model runs while the switch is on.

Gather plumber putty, a screwdriver set, a hex key, pliers, a bucket and the parts that come with the unit. A new power cord kit may be needed if your unit ships without one.

Installation steps in order

Power and water off

Turn off the breaker and close the supply valves. Test the outlet with a voltage tester.

Remove the old parts

Disconnect the trap and the old flange or sink strainer.

Mount the new flange

Seat the flange in a ring of plumber putty and tighten the mounting assembly from below.

Hang the unit

Lift the disposal onto the mounting ring and twist it until it locks.

Connect the drain and dishwasher hose

Attach the discharge elbow, reconnect the trap and attach the dishwasher hose after you knock out the plug.

Test

Restore power and water, check for leaks and run the unit with cold water.

Common installation mistakes

These trip up most first-timers.

  • Forgetting the dishwasher knockoutA plastic plug inside the inlet blocks the dishwasher drain until you knock it out.
  • Not enough putty under the flangeA thin seal leaks around the sink within weeks.
  • Overtightening the mounting ringCracks the flange or sink and cause leaks.
  • Misaligned trapThe discharge elbow must line up with the trap arm or the pipes will strain and leak.
  • No switch or a shared circuitThe wiring needs to be safe and, for some installs, an electrician should handle it.

Lenexa notes: wiring, permits and licensed help

Lenexa has adopted the 2017 National Electrical Code (NEC) and the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC). New wiring for a disposal falls under the electrical rules, and Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can tell you whether your project needs a permit. The office also answers at permits@lenexa.com.

For wiring work, use an electrician licensed through Johnson County, and use a licensed plumber for plumbing changes. You can check any contractor at cls.jocogov.org or by calling Johnson County Contractor Licensing at 913-715-2233.

Neighboring Overland Park follows its own permit rules, so call that city if you live there.

Choosing a unit that fits your kitchen

Horsepower is the number most shoppers compare, but the grind chamber, the motor mount and the noise insulation matter as much. A larger household that cooks often benefits from a higher horsepower motor, while a one or two person kitchen does fine with a mid-range unit.

Measure the space under the sink before you buy. A deep disposal can hit the trap or a sink bracing bar, and a dishwasher connection must have room for the hose. Cabinets with a built-in shelf or a filter system under the sink may leave little clearance.

Look at the warranty and the parts available. A disposal with a long warranty and a replaceable cord kit costs a little more up front and less over its life.

After the install: using it well

Run cold water before, during and for about fifteen seconds after each use. Cold water keeps grease solid so it moves through the blades instead of coating the pipe.

Avoid grease, coffee grounds, pasta, rice, potato peels and fibrous vegetables. They cause most jams and clogs. If the kitchen line slows despite careful use, read our garbage disposal repair page or ask about drain cleaning.

Keep the hex key that came with the unit. Many jams free up when you turn the flywheel from the bottom.

Questions homeowners ask

If you have an outlet and switch and a standard sink opening, many homeowners can. New wiring or damaged drain pipes are good reasons to call a licensed pro.
