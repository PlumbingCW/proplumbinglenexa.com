Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Lenexa, KS

A burst pipe can put hundreds of gallons into a house in an hour. We repair burst, cracked and frozen pipes in Lenexa, KS around the clock, and the first job is always stopping the water.

Burst pipe: stop the water in the next five minutes

Speed limits the damage. Work through this list in order.

  1. Find the main water shutoff, often near the meter or where the line enters the basement wall, and turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the lines and relieve pressure.
  3. If water is near outlets or the electrical panel, do not step into it. Shut the breaker only if you can do it from a dry spot.
  4. Move furniture, rugs and boxes out of the wet area, and lift photographs of the damage for your insurer.
  5. Call us. Dispatch will tell you how long the crew needs and what else to shut off.

Why pipes burst in Lenexa homes

Most burst pipes come from one of three causes. Freezing is the most visible, because water expands about nine percent as it turns to ice and splits the pipe wall. Corrosion and old joints fail without any cold at all. And sudden pressure spikes, such as a water hammer or a failed pressure regulator, can pop a weak fitting.

Lenexa winters bring hard freezes, and pipes in exterior walls, garages, crawl spaces and unheated basement corners are the usual victims. The break often shows after the thaw, when water starts flowing through a pipe that has already split.

If a pipe has frozen but not yet burst, keep the faucet slightly open and aim a heat source at the cold section. Never use an open flame, and call us if the line will not thaw safely.

Signs of a cracked or failing pipe before it bursts

A pipe usually warns you before it lets go.

  • Water stains or damp drywallA slow weep from a pinhole shows up as a brown ring on a ceiling or a damp spot on a wall.
  • Pressure that drops suddenlyA big drop at every tap may mean a main line break between the meter and the house.
  • Rust-colored waterGalvanized steel corrodes from inside, narrowing the pipe and weakening the wall.
  • Banging or hammering when a valve closesShock can stress joints and old solder, so it is worth fixing.
  • Frost on an exposed pipeIce inside means the pipe is under strain and could split as it thaws.
Frozen line and no water at all?

Do not hold a torch to it. We thaw lines safely and repair any damage found along the way.

Water service line breaks between the meter and the house

The water service line runs from the meter to the house, and a break on it can flood a yard or crawl space. The signs are a sudden drop in pressure, a wet patch above the line or a loud rush near the meter.

WaterOne publishes a Service Line Lookup Tool that shows the material of the line at each address, and it states that it has no record of lead service lines in its distribution system. Check the tool before a repair so we know what we are connecting to.

A repair or replacement on the service line may need a permit or a right-of-way permit depending on where the work sits. Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can tell you which applies.

After the repair: drying, documenting and insurance

We stop the leak and make the pipe sound. Drying and restoring the building is a separate job, and a water mitigation company usually handles that. Do not wait for it. Wet drywall and insulation start growing mold within a day or two.

Photograph every wet area before things are moved, keep damaged materials until your insurer says otherwise, and ask us for a written summary of the cause of the break. A sudden burst is often covered, while a slow leak or neglected maintenance may not be.
