Water Softener and Filtration Installation in Lenexa, KS

Scale on faucets, spots on glass and a film in the shower are all signs of minerals in your water. We install and service water softeners and filtration systems in Lenexa, KS, and we start by testing.

Start with the number: test your tap water

Water treatment should follow a test, not a sales pitch. Hardness, iron, chlorine taste and other issues each need a different tool, and one system rarely fixes all of them.

WaterOne, which supplies Lenexa, publishes an annual water quality report with hardness listed in the data. Hardness appears in parts per million or grains per gallon, and you convert ppm to grains by dividing by 17.1. Look up the current report before you decide on a softener size.

You can also test your own tap with a test strip or a lab kit. If you have a private well or a different source, test that water directly.

Signs of hard water or other water quality issues

Minerals leave visible clues.

  • White scale on faucets and shower headsCalcium and magnesium deposits build up as water dries.
  • Spots on glasses and dishesDissolved minerals leave residue after the water evaporates.
  • Dry skin or stiff laundryMineral-heavy water can leave soap residue.
  • A water heater that rumblesScale at the bottom of the tank can cause popping noises and reduce efficiency. See water heater repair.
  • Chlorine taste or smellA carbon filter can improve taste and odor.

Softener or filter: which one solves which problem

These are different tools for different problems.

ProblemSystemWhat it does
Scale and hardnessWater softener.Swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium.
Chlorine taste and odorCarbon filter.Adsorbs chlorine and some organic compounds.
SedimentSediment filter.Traps particles before they reach fixtures.
Drinking water improvementReverse osmosis at one tap.Filters drinking water at a single faucet.
Iron and certain stainsSpecialty filter.Needs a lab test to pick the right one.

A softener does not filter drinking water on its own.

What a softener does to your plumbing and appliances

Hard water leaves scale inside water heaters, on heating elements and in pipes. Over time it reduces efficiency and shortens appliance life. A softener reduces scale buildup and helps appliances work as designed.

A softener needs a drain for its regeneration cycle, a power outlet nearby and salt or potassium to keep it working. It should be placed where the main line enters the house and before the water heater.

If you have a tankless unit, scale is a real concern, because small passages clog easily. Our tankless water heater page explains maintenance.

Permits, drains and salt discharge

A softener connects to the water main and needs a drain, and Lenexa has adopted the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Lenexa Community Development at 913-477-7725 can confirm whether your installation needs a permit.

Regeneration brine goes down the drain. Some communities limit salt discharge, so ask before you choose a model. We set the unit to regenerate only when needed, which saves salt and water.

For a home on a septic system, check with your septic professional first, since a softener adds water volume to the tank.
