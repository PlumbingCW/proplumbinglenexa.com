Gas Water Heater Thermostat Replacement: A Lenexa, KS Homeowner Guide

By the Lenexa Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026.

On a gas water heater, the thermostat is built into the gas control valve at the bottom of the tank. When it fails, the water runs too hot, stays cold or the burner never lights. Replacing it means working on a gas appliance, so it is a job for a licensed plumber for most Lenexa homeowners.

This guide explains how to tell a thermostat problem from a pilot problem, what the repair involves and when a new heater makes more sense.

What the thermostat on a gas water heater actually is

People say thermostat, but on most residential gas heaters the dial you turn is part of a combined gas control valve. That one part senses tank temperature, opens and closes the gas to the burner and holds the pilot safety. If one function fails, the whole valve is usually replaced as a unit.

Electric heaters are different, with separate thermostats inside access panels. Do not follow electric heater advice on a gas model, because gas parts need different handling and a leak can be dangerous.

A gas heater also has a thermocouple or flame sensor that proves the pilot is lit. Many "thermostat" complaints turn out to be a worn thermocouple or a dirty pilot, and those are much cheaper repairs.

Symptoms and what they usually mean

Match what you see before you decide on a repair.

  • Water too hot even at the lowest settingThe control valve may not be shutting off the burner. Turn the dial down and call, since overheated water can scald.
  • Lukewarm or cold water, burner runningA failed dip tube, heavy sediment or a weak burner can cause this, so the thermostat may not be to blame.
  • Burner does not light, pilot is outA bad thermocouple or a draft is common. A pilot that will not stay lit after a new thermocouple points to the valve.
  • Dial feels loose or does not change the temperatureThe valve may have failed internally.
  • Gas smell near the heaterLeave the house and call the gas utility from outside. Do not try to fix it yourself.

Before you touch anything: safety steps

These steps apply to every gas heater problem.

  1. If you smell gas, do not flip switches or use a lighter. Leave and call your gas utility and 911 from outside.
  2. Turn the gas control dial to pilot or off and close the gas shutoff on the supply line only if you can do it safely.
  3. Never bypass or tape over a safety device to get hot water back.
  4. Check the temperature setting. Many manufacturers suggest 120 degrees Fahrenheit as a safe starting point, so read the label on your tank.
  5. Photograph the data label with the model and serial number before you call.

A good rule is to compare the repair quote with the age of the tank. Putting a new gas valve on a heater near the end of its life rarely pays off.

What a replacement visit looks like

The plumber shuts off the gas and water, drains enough of the tank to work and disconnects the old valve. The new valve is fitted, the gas connections are tested for leaks and the pilot is relit. The heater is then checked for temperature, venting and the relief valve.

A good visit includes a look at the flue and the draft, since a poor draft can mimic a thermostat failure. It also includes a check of the temperature and pressure relief valve, which protects the tank from overpressure.

If the tank is sound but sediment has built up, a flush at the same visit can improve performance. Our water heater repair page explains what else we check.

Should you do it yourself?

Replacing the valve involves gas piping, venting and a pressurized tank. A small mistake can cause a gas leak, carbon monoxide or scalding water. Some jurisdictions require a permit and licensed work for gas, and insurers may question damage from an unpermitted repair.

What you can do safely is check the temperature setting, look at the pilot window and flush the tank according to the label. Anything involving gas connections is best left to a plumber.
